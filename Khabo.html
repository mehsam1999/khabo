Step 1 · Compare two apps

Both apps make you browse. Neither lets you just say what you want.

Pain points below are hypotheses from app-store reviews and hands-on use. Edit them to match the evidence you gathered in the assessment.

Foodpanda

Where users get stuck

  • Long lists and filters. Finding "spicy, under 500, for two" takes many taps.
  • Fees and totals appear late, at checkout.
  • Items go out of stock after the order is placed.
  • Delivery estimates are a single number with no confidence.
Foodi

Where users get stuck

  • Smaller catalogue, so search must work harder to feel useful.
  • Basic keyword search. Bangla and mixed-language queries often fail.
  • Fewer discovery tools for first-time users.
  • Support is reactive. Problems are fixed after the order goes wrong.

Product decision

A smaller app wins by being easier, not bigger. The AI Concierge removes the browsing step and makes cost and time predictable. Those two things are where a challenger can feel better on day one without more restaurants.

How the answer is built
1
Read the sentence. Budget, headcount, cuisine, spice level and time limit come from plain text, including Bangla digits.
2
Filter the menu. Items that break the budget or the time limit are removed first.
3
Price it honestly. Item × people + delivery + 5% VAT, shown before the customer taps anything.
4
Rank and explain. Rating, fit and distance decide the order. Each pick says why.
5
Protect the order. If an item sells out, a close substitute is offered at once.
Why each feature exists
  • Natural-language order: removes the browse-and-filter step.
  • Upfront total: no checkout surprise, so fewer abandoned carts.
  • Time range + confidence: sets honest expectations and cuts "where is my order" contacts.
  • Smart substitute: saves orders that would otherwise be cancelled.
